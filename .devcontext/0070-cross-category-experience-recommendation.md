# Advisory work record 0070: Cross-category experience

- Date: 2026-10-15
- Status at creation: Recommendation only; implementation not requested

## Request and findings

Michelle explained that her agency work spans technology, automotive, consumer
goods, telecommunications, logistics and non-profit, naming IBM, BMW (written
BWM in the request), Kraft Foods, Rogers, Canada Post, Purolator, OLG and SickKids
Foundation. She wants visitors to recognize her breadth beyond financial services
and asked whether and where to incorporate the experience.

Read the homepage, existing logo component and records
[0014](0014-company-logos.md), [0015](0015-company-logo-carousel.md) and
[0041](0041-balanced-experience-and-section-introductions.md). The current
experience strip combines RBC, HSBC and agency employers Publicis, JWT and FCB;
it does not name her agency clients. The first two leadership stories explicitly
feature banks, so the agency breadth needs earlier visible context.

## Recommendation and rationale

Add a compact, static brand-experience block within The Throughline, after the
personal narrative and before the next section. A small heading such as
A perspective shaped across industries, one short sentence explaining agency
experience, and eight restrained typographic brand names would make the breadth
visible early while preserving the main section headline. Use existing colours,
wrapping and readable type rather than a second moving logo strip.

Label it Selected brand experience through agency roles to distinguish client
work from employment. Keep the existing employer carousel's identity clear.
Support it with a short narrative sentence linking agency breadth to the
executive banking experience. Over time, one concrete agency example within an
appropriate existing leadership story would substantiate the wider experience.
No new fifth leadership pillar is necessary for this recommendation.

Pentagram's work page exposes sectors alongside project work; this is a concrete
example of making industry range visible. The proposed placement and visual
treatment for Michelle are design judgment, not a measured visitor outcome.
