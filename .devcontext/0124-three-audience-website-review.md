## Audience assessment

- Executive recruiter: positioning signals marketing breadth and a distinctive
  perspective, but specific mandate, career progression, responsibility and scale
  are not immediately clear. Employer/client distinctions are now correctly
  labelled. Films substantiate creative work, not by themselves executive business
  accountability or enterprise transformation. Agile contribution/outcome is a
  comparatively concrete example, but lives inside the fourth disclosure. Executive
  enquiry exists below the stories rather than early in the journey.
- Advisory client: first three offerings identify useful problems, but the shape
  of an engagement and what a client would leave with remain unclear. Fourth
  Challenger conversations offer describes a developing space rather than a
  specific client need or deliverable. Do not imply existing advisory engagements.
- Event organizer: three themes give possible subject areas, but the site lacks
  specific session ideas, audience/takeaways and evidence of Michelle presenting.
  Campaign films are not speaking samples. No claim that she lacks speaking
  experience outside the website is justified.

## Five recommendations, in proposed priority order

1. Clarify the executive mandate immediately. Content: an explicit executive
   marketing descriptor and concise bridge from agency/brand leadership to brand,
   customer growth and marketing transformation; target role/scope only after
   confirmation. Design: a quiet descriptor beside the current personal headline,
   not a competing headline or redesign. Present change leadership as an evidenced
   capability; do not imply unproven enterprise-wide change remit or titles held.
2. Make leadership evidence decision-ready. Content: relevant roles and shareable
   scope, business challenge, personal decision/tradeoff, action and verified
   outcome. Qualitative outcomes are valid; do not publish confidential resume
   metrics. Existing agile story is usable evidence, not sole global ownership.
   Design: expose a concise contribution/outcome outside each closed accordion;
   keep film/narrative detail inside. Reframe the RBC partnership example precisely
   so a campaign does not stand in for operational enterprise reinvention.
3. Give executive visitors the shortest route to proof and contact. Content:
   optional request for a reviewed executive profile rather than automatically
   publishing the resume. Design/flow: hero CTA to Leadership in Action, evidence
   before service offerings, compact navigation available from the top, and an
   earlier Executive opportunities link to the existing preselected form. Preserve
   a single dominant hero CTA. Reduce repeated setup prose and the prominence of
   empty/future writing content until actual writing exists.
4. Turn advisory categories into understandable engagements. Content: for each
   of the strongest offers, explain when to call Michelle, proposed way of working
   and tangible output. Examples are proposed service shapes, never past client
   claims. Consider using verified team/ways-of-working experience for the vague
   fourth card. Design: consistent short problem/approach/output rhythm and an
   advisory-labelled invitation to the same form, rather than more decorative cards.
5. Make the speaking invitation specific and assessable. Content: two or three
   proposed session ideas based on actual experience, who benefits, practical
   takeaways, formats she is willing to offer, concise bio and genuine speaking
   sample if available. Distinguish proposed talks from prior bookings. Design:
   a compact block near thought leadership with a direct speaking enquiry action;
   use the existing form and its audience/topic/format/timing prompt. Do not add
   fictitious bookings, testimonials, a reel assembled from ads, or published work.
