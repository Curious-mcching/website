# Leadership positioning review

Reviewed 17 October 2026. This is a qualitative sample of six readable postings
on employers' own recruitment sites, across four organizations. It identifies
useful positioning signals, rather than establishing what most employers require
or assessing suitability for any particular vacancy. Three roles are from one
employer, so their priorities should not be counted as three independent market
signals. Closed or unreadable postings were excluded from the sample.

## The strongest story

Michelle's breadth becomes more compelling when it explains her contribution:
connecting customer understanding and brand strategy with commercial priorities,
then bringing teams together to turn strategy into action. Agency and enterprise
experience, multiple industries, marketing disciplines and international
collaboration are the foundations of that perspective.

The website already communicates curiosity, creative courage and human leadership.
Keep those qualities: they make it personal and relevant to fellow thinkers as
well as prospective employers and advisory clients. Strengthen the connection to
decisions, execution and outcomes rather than adding a longer list of channels.

Across this sample, my interpretation is that a persuasive executive narrative
needs to connect four things: the business opportunity, the strategic choice,
the people and capabilities needed to execute it, and the resulting value.
Breadth is strongest when it helps explain those choices.

## Whole-site review

### Hero and throughline

Keep the personal opening and its balance of challenger thinking, commercial
discipline and human leadership. It is distinctive and consistent with the
leadership contribution described above. Corrected “unlock growth” to “unlocked
growth” in the experience paragraph.

### Many perspectives. One throughline.

Updated the introduction to connect breadth with brand growth, organizational
change and coordinated execution. The three columns now use comparable-length
paragraphs: customer-led strategic judgment across industries; integrated brand
building and marketing modernization; and translating international collaboration
into locally relevant decisions. Creator partnerships and AI discoverability
remain explicit. The selective capability labels, industry categories and market
list remain intact. No new credentials or performance claims were added.

### Where we could make a difference

The existing growth/customer strategy, brand/go-to-market and marketing
transformation offerings already cover relevant leadership priorities. The
transformation card connects teams, capabilities, technology, investment,
experimentation and measurement. Keep this focused structure rather than adding
another service card for every skill mentioned in a vacancy.

### Leadership in Action

- **Challenger strategy:** the HSBC films make the story tangible. A brief
  explanation of Michelle's strategic choice, personal responsibility and a
  verified result would connect creative courage to business value.
- **Enterprise reinvention:** the RBC partnership film demonstrates creative and
  partnership thinking within a large organization. On its own it does not
  establish organization-wide operational transformation. Future supporting
  detail should explain what internal approach changed and how it was adopted.
- **Customer-led transformation:** onboarding and engagement modernization is a
  useful example of connecting customer insight, data, technology and teams.
  Strengthen it with the specific decision, cross-functional challenge and
  observed change in the experience.
- **Human leadership:** this is a natural place to demonstrate clarity through
  uncertainty, team capability, alignment and sustained change. A concrete
  example would make the philosophy easier to assess.

### Thought leadership, writing and contact

Keep the three evolving areas of inquiry. Distinguish questions being explored
from expertise demonstrated in the leadership stories. Keep the truthful empty
writing state until there are actual papers or perspectives to publish. The
contact invitation already makes advisory, consulting, speaking and executive
leadership opportunities clear without compromising the site's personal voice.

## The next improvement needs evidence

The biggest remaining gap is proof of scope and outcomes, rather than more
positioning language. For one or two stories, collect:

1. The business/customer problem and the constraint.
2. Michelle's own accountability, decision and contribution.
3. Relevant scale: team, markets, budget or organizational reach, where shareable.
4. How colleagues were aligned and how a new approach was adopted.
5. A verified outcome: a business/customer measure, a capability improvement,
   or a specific, supportable qualitative result.

These are evidence prompts, not claims to publish without confirmation. No team
sizes, budgets, commercial results, formal change credentials, global job titles
or specialist technical capabilities have been invented. Collaborating with
international teams should remain distinct from having led every listed market.

## Implementation follow-up: 17 October 2026

Michelle approved the recommendations. The homepage opening now names brand
building, marketing modernization and leading teams through change. The experience
overview explains how agency and brand-side leadership connect creative ambition,
customer understanding and execution within complex organizations.

Each expanded leadership story now has a concise contribution summary before
its narrative. The HSBC, RBC and customer-modernization summaries use the existing
account of Michelle's role and strategic focus; they do not claim new results.

Michelle supplied a concrete Human Leadership example: drawing on agile coaching
and design thinking training, she helped move marketing from traditional to agile
ways of working as an agile change champion in one of three markets leading a
global initiative. She adapted the approach for marketing, created a monday.com
board to visualize work, and established shared ownership with product owners and
Scrum Masters. The published outcome is qualitative: greater transparency and
stronger visibility of the team's work. This addresses the earlier recommendation
for a concrete people/change example without adding unsupported commercial claims.

Michelle raised concern about publishing company performance figures. Those
figures remain off the public website. No assumption has been made about permission
to disclose numbers in her resume, and no confidentiality/legal conclusion is
implied by their omission. The site can communicate her leadership contribution
and the qualitative outcomes she has chosen to share.
